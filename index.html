Internal Resistance Lab

A cell has to push charge through itself as well as round the circuit. Its internal resistance stays the same, but the volts lost inside it depend on the current.

Terminal p.d. against current

Try this
  • Open the switch. With no current there are no lost volts, so the voltmeter reads the EMF.
  • Turn the resistance down. The current rises, and so do the lost volts. Did r change?
  • Swap to the old cell at the same resistor setting. Same EMF, bigger r: what happens to the terminal p.d.?

Drag the variable resistor's contact and click the switch. Each dot stands for many electrons. They repel each other, so they spread out where they can and crowd up where the cell pushes them. Bluer wire means more crowding. The voltmeter compares the crowding at the two terminals.
